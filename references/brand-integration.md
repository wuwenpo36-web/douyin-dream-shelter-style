## Soft Integration Model

Use this sequence:

`credible pain point -> relevant environment -> natural use -> visible response -> story continues`

The product or service must solve something already present in the story. Do not stop the episode to demonstrate a feature in isolation.

Examples:

- air purifier beside a living-room sofa after the character settles; the character turns it on in first person; restrained air-flow visualization appears; PM and formaldehyde values decrease as a response, not as the scene's only content;
- a retail shelter receives wet gear, offers drying, hot water, food processing, charging, and a safe place to rest;
- chocolate extends the account's “smoothness” into a slide, recline, silk transition, music ritual, or flowing interior route.

## Product-Context Plausibility

Check category fit before designing the scene.

- Air purification belongs near occupied living zones, bedrooms, renovation concerns, smoke, odor, allergens, or enclosed spaces—not beside arbitrary textiles with no air-quality motivation.
- Food and drink belong in recovery, preparation, sharing, or supply moments.
- Membership and service benefits belong at the moment they remove friction, not as a floating price card.
- Personal-care products belong in changing, bathing, rest, or recovery spaces.

If the context is weak, change the space or motivation before writing the prompt.

## Convert Claims Into Experiences

Avoid ordinary shelves, long metadata overlays, and generic inspection panels. Embody the claim spatially.

- Source quality -> indoor ecological valley with real crops, water, source basket, origin tag, and a walkable path.
- Careful service -> protected living rain court with hot drink, food handling, packaging, charging, hand-washing, and a resting nook.
- Membership -> private, fully equipped refuge where supplies, dry clothing, charged phone, food, pet care, and rest arrive together.
- Softness -> a landing surface, reclining movement, textile response, or gentle physical catch.
- Adaptive sleep support -> shallow tactile compression, slow damped recovery, a supported side-sleep posture, then one continuous turn to back sleeping with pressure transferring to the central support zone.
- Fresh air -> clean directional airflow, intake and outlet behavior, and values changing after use.

Show the service changing the character's physical state: wet to dry, cold to warm, hungry to fed, exposed to protected, low battery to charged, overwhelmed to calm.

## Demonstrate Material Claims Through Different Loads

Do not use one exaggerated deformation to prove every softness claim. Separate three loads:

1. **Hand touch:** a wide, very shallow local change in highlight, fabric tension, and curvature; no deep palm print or finger wells.
2. **Body support:** a larger but controlled compression under the head or torso, with the body remaining visibly above the surface.
3. **Posture adaptation:** pressure migrates continuously when the person changes position; the unloaded area recovers slowly while the new support area compresses.

For sleep products, show alignment and comfort rather than only softness. In side sleeping, shoulders remain on the mattress and the pillow fills the shoulder-neck gap. During a single natural turn to back sleeping, the head stays in contact with the surface and settles into the central support zone while the neck remains neutral.

If the product reference includes multiple products, models, furniture, a studio background, or a lifestyle scene, inherit only the named target product and lock the object count. Exact brand text is not reliably guaranteed by generative video alone; keep the correct physical brand area visible and use post-production text when delivery requires perfect legibility.

## Preserve The Account Format

Keep multiple shelter spaces and a connected journey. The integration should occupy one or more real rooms rather than replace the episode with a conventional store or commercial.

When the account convention avoids people, do not add customers, staff, farmers, or crowds. Use:

- a friendly robot or mascot;
- self-moving baskets;
- conveyor or rail systems hidden in planting;
- automated doors and service counters;
- objects, lighting, and sound that respond to the protagonist.

## Virtual Service Characters

Give the robot or mascot only actions that make service visible:

- collect wet gear;
- deliver a towel or hot drink;
- guide the character;
- present a basket;
- charge a phone;
- return processed food or packaged goods.

Keep the design friendly and premium. Avoid a busy humanoid employee replacement, excessive dialogue, or brand exposition.

## Client-Facing Outline Style

Write clear numbered sections with these fields when useful:

- scene purpose;
- environment and spatial idea;
- character action;
- service or product expression;
- visual contrast or memory point;
- transition to the next space.

Use concrete production language. Avoid poetic filler such as “watching a meal travel backward through time” unless it is a literal visual mechanic.

State which exact classic ad or brand memory is being referenced when the project depends on recognition.

## Ending Design

Prefer an emotional or object-led ending:

- character sits with a hot drink while the storm remains outside;
- music starts and the room settles;
- a robot places the last item on the table;
- a membership card appears among the already solved needs;
- a pet settles beside the character;
- a product remains naturally in the final still life.

Avoid a separate information slate, bullet list, hard call to action, or sudden sales voice unless the client explicitly requires it.

For a recovery-focused final beat, a restrained external camera may be allowed after first-person exploration only when the camera follows a continuous, traceable path around a stable product or bed-edge anchor and gradually reveals the same protagonist. This is an experience proof, not a free-floating beauty orbit or a hidden cut.

## Proven Branded Patterns

### Tianhong Quality Shelter

Use a route such as:

1. rain buffer service zone;
2. source ecological valley;
3. umbrella-inspired life garden with real services;
4. private “生活有光” recovery suite.

Keep the ecological valley large and immersive, with real crops and source evidence. Make the rain garden visually distinct from the valley. Make the final suite private and fully equipped rather than a shared lounge.

### Dove Tribute Modules

- Classic recline: after eating chocolate, the seated body tilts smoothly toward a dog and settles into a hug; use only when the intended classic ad is named in the outline.
- Rain and music: moderate safety window, gray-blue rain outside, warm wood and textiles inside, vinyl or unbranded music interface, product at real size beside the listening ritual.
- Silk display: product in a refined window with one real dark-brown silk ribbon continuing into wall, rug, or bedding.
- Chocolate slide: treat “silky” as continuous physical motion into or around the shelter; keep liquid behavior plausible and the route safe.

### Product Fidelity

When references provide packaging, bottle, card, or prop construction, preserve it exactly. Keep hand-held scale believable. Do not invent wording, claims, medical effects, dates, or logos.
