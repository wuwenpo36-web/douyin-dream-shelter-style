# Interiors And Image Edits

Use this reference for shelter interior planning, atmosphere, night scenes, room prompts, safe layout, and minimal-change image edits.

## The Three-Space Route

Plan a connected emotional sequence:

1. **Buffer:** remove the exterior problem—water, mud, cold, damaged gear, fatigue, low battery, hunger, or contamination.
2. **Signature room:** deliver the episode's most memorable spatial idea and its main emotional contrast.
3. **Private recovery:** provide sleep, bathing, music, gaming, reading, viewing, or quiet rest.

Connect rooms through a real route: a curved corridor, protected garden, bridge, spiral stair, sliding wall, rain court, or shared material transition. Avoid three unrelated images that feel like different houses.

## Safety And Circulation

Leave a visible human-width path from the camera to the next space. Furniture should frame movement, not block it.

Prefer:

- moderate windows with thick structural reveals;
- laminated or segmented glazing;
- shutters or protective layers;
- dry entry benches, boot wash, drains, hooks, and towel storage;
- emergency power, charging, water, food, medical storage, and communication equipment;
- handrails and non-slip floors;
- protected doors with rounded, well-finished detailing.

Avoid:

- wall-to-wall glass with no protection;
- an oversized sofa filling the foreground;
- a bed or table sealing the only route;
- a private sanctuary laid out like a public lobby or shared hotel lounge;
- decorative “safety” elements that have no function.

## Composition And Scale

Use human eye-level architecture photography, not a dollhouse, top-down plan, or miniature set.

Create richness through section and sequence:

- ceiling changes;
- alcoves and built-ins;
- a framed focal window;
- raised or recessed zones only when circulation remains clear;
- curved joinery, niches, shelves, and layered thresholds;
- one dominant feature plus supporting details.

Avoid both failure extremes:

- **too empty:** a wide rectangular box with furniture pushed to walls;
- **too cramped:** every surface occupied, no standing space, no visual hierarchy.

## Theme Translation

Translate a source object into architecture rather than literal décor.

- Mango: soft fruit-slice curves, cream and warm yellow, leaf-green accents, one small tree or garden, rounded joinery.
- Pinecone: layered shingle or scale rhythm, deep brown timber, resin-gold light, cone-shaped cushion or acoustic panel.
- Drift bottle: curved glass only where safe, cork acoustic material, brass bands, blue condensation accents, bottle-neck corridors.
- Pizza: radial ceiling or floor logic, crust-like padded edges, herb greenhouse, warm terracotta and mozzarella cream—not giant cartoon toppings everywhere.
- Scorpion: protected segmented ribs, controlled tail-arc geometry, dark refined metal, burgundy or amber accents—not horror props.
- Chocolate: satin curves, cocoa brown, ivory, gold reflections, silk and flowing circulation—not a room coated in wet chocolate.

Use only a few unmistakable themed details in each room.

## Color And Material Systems

Use one of these structures:

- one main color + two secondary colors;
- two main colors + one accent color.

Balance warm and cool contrast. Let exterior blue-gray reflections reach only window reveals, nearby floor, or furniture edges. Keep the interior's material body warm.

Good material layers include textured plaster, warm timber, wool, boucle, linen, leather, matte metal, stone, acoustic cork, and selective glossy details.

Avoid monochrome black, monochrome yellow, all-white showroom styling, many competing stained-glass colors, random neon, and distressed surfaces when the brief asks for premium comfort.

## Physically Sourced Lighting

Name every important light source. Do not rely on glowing walls or unexplained strips.

Use these useful targets as guidance:

- living room: about 200–300 lux;
- kitchen and preparation: about 400–500 lux;
- reading or desk area: about 350–450 lux;
- bedroom resting state: about 100–150 lux;
- warm residential sources: about 2700–3000K.

Layer light through:

- overcast daylight from a controlled window;
- pendants over tables or counters;
- wall sconces at transitions;
- floor or table lamps at seating;
- fireplace or candlelight as a low secondary source;
- task lighting inside storage or preparation zones;
- a restrained RGB or neon accent when the concept needs energy.

Allow light to spread and bounce realistically across plaster, wood, textiles, and metal. Preserve shadow detail so the room remains legible.

## Night Atmosphere

Describe the whole room at night:

- exterior: rain, fog, snow, sea, trees, or city lights;
- main interior source: bedside lamps, pendants, sconces, fireplace, or task lights;
- secondary source: record player indicator, screen glow, candles, or subtle ceiling feature;
- recovery ritual: hot drink, music, blankets, reading, food, shower, or charging;
- acoustic cues: rain muffled by thick glass, fire crackle, record texture, ventilation, or distant wind.

Do not equate atmosphere with darkness. Keep the route, furniture, and themed details readable.

## Protected Semi-Open Spaces

Make gardens, water courts, bridges, terraces, and rain rooms safe through deep eaves, transparent roofs, wind screens, drainage, anti-slip surfaces, and closable protection.

If the interior must remain dry, keep all rain outside the envelope. Show safety through water flowing over exterior glazing, garden plants bending beyond the barrier, and warm dry surfaces inside.

For umbrella or canopy ceilings, use a controlled palette and clear structural logic. A few translucent, lightly tinted glass colors work better than many textured panels.

## Image-Edit Discipline

For a minimal edit, begin with the change and then lock the rest:

1. Identify the exact target region.
2. State the replacement geometry, material, scale, and lighting.
3. Require perspective, occlusion, reflection, and shadow to match the original.
4. Explicitly preserve camera, crop, architecture, furniture, weather, color grade, and every unrelated object.

Examples:

- Replace two small turntables below the window with one large, unmistakable vinyl record player; preserve the cabinet and all other room details.
- Remove the hatch under a vehicle body; reconstruct one continuous hull surface with matching seams and reflections.
- Remove a door handle and place one clear keyhole at the same functional height; preserve the door motif.

Do not restyle the entire image when the user asks for one local change.

## Interior Quality Checklist

- Is the route visible and walkable?
- Does the room have a focal point other than a huge window?
- Are windows moderate, protected, and psychologically safe?
- Are useful facilities visible?
- Does the room feel warm without becoming uniformly yellow?
- Are all important light sources physically present?
- Is the themed identity architectural rather than toy-like?
- Is there enough detail for richness but enough negative space for calm?
- Does this room look compositionally different from the previous room?
- Does the exterior threat remain visible enough to strengthen the safety contrast?
